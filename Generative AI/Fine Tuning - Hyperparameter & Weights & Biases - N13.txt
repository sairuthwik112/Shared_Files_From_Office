Warmup Ratio

What it is:

Controls how long the model starts learning slowly.
Learning rate does not immediately jump to the target value.
It gradually increases at the beginning of training.

Why it is needed:

At the start, the model weights are not adapted to your dataset.
A large learning rate can make training unstable.
Loss may increase suddenly or training may fail.

Example:

Learning rate = 0.0001
Warmup ratio = 0.01
Total steps = 10,000

Then:

First 100 steps are warmup steps.
Learning rate gradually goes from very small value to 0.0001.

Simple idea:

Start slowly before running fast.

Cosine Scheduler

What it is:

A strategy that changes learning rate during training.
After warmup, learning rate gradually decreases.

Why it is needed:

Early training needs larger updates.
Later training needs smaller and more precise updates.

What happens:

Beginning: learning rate is high.
Middle: learning rate slowly decreases.
End: learning rate becomes very small.

Benefit:

Stable convergence.
Better final accuracy.
Commonly used for LLM fine-tuning.

Simple idea:

Learn aggressively at first.
Fine tune carefully at the end.

Weight Decay

What it is:

A regularization technique.
Prevents weights from becoming too large.

Problem without weight decay:

Some weights may become extremely large.
Model may memorize training data.
Overfitting increases.

What it does:

Adds a penalty when weights grow too much.
Encourages simpler models.

Benefit:

Better generalization.
Less overfitting.

Simple idea:

Prevent weights from becoming too powerful.

Dropout

What it is:

Randomly disables some neurons during training.

Example:

Dropout = 0.1
About 10% of neurons are temporarily ignored.

Why it is needed:

Prevents the model from relying too much on a few neurons.
Forces the model to learn multiple patterns.

Benefit:

Reduced memorization.
Better performance on new data.

Important:

Dropout is only used during training.
During inference, all neurons are active.

Simple idea:

Hide some neurons so the model cannot cheat by memorizing.

Early Stopping

What it is:

A technique used to stop training before overfitting starts.

What we monitor:

Validation Loss

Example:

Epoch 1:

Validation Loss = 1.2

Epoch 2:

Validation Loss = 0.8

Epoch 3:

Validation Loss = 0.6

Epoch 4:

Validation Loss = 0.7

Epoch 5:

Validation Loss = 1.0

Best model:

Epoch 3

Why?

Lowest validation loss.

Even though Epoch 5 trained more, it performed worse on unseen data.

Simple idea:

Stop when validation performance is best.

AdamW Optimizer

What it is:

The component that actually updates weights.

Process:

Loss → Gradients → AdamW → New Weights

What AdamW does:

Uses gradients to decide how much each weight should change.
Uses learning rate.
Applies weight decay automatically.

Why AdamW is popular:

Faster convergence.
More stable training.
Works very well for transformers and LLMs.

What AdamW receives:

Weight values.
Gradients from backpropagation.

What AdamW outputs:

Updated weights.

Simple idea:

AdamW is the mechanic that adjusts the weights after backpropagation tells what is wrong.
